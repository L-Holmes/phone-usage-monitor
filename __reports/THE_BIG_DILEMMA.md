Drop / don't build:

The bypass-detection surveillance — flagging that someone's pricing a new phone, hunting for a VPN, finding a shop. It's invasive, it's futile, and "harvest more user data so they can't escape" directly contradicts the local-only, zero-knowledge, non-triggering architecture your own first doc recommended. You'd be building the opposite of the trust the product runs on.
Continuous screenshotting and total content-reading. The OS is fighting you for a reason — let it.

Build instead — friction and self-binding, not detection:
The legitimate, effective version of "make it harder" is the commitment device: the user, in a calm moment, binds their own future self. This sidesteps the entire "we can't see the screen" problem, because you don't need to know what they're looking at — only to raise the cost of the impulsive path on contexts they chose to flag.

Greylist → friction, not judgment. You weren't sure what to do on greylisted sites. Don't try to detect content (you can't). Apply a pre-committed pause to the whole context: open a user-flagged risky app → forced 30–60s wait + "want to urge-surf first?" The user set the list when calm; the app just honours it when they're not.
Pre-emptive JITAI on time + self-reported context — the high-value lever, no surveillance needed. Their 11pm window, their home-alone Sunday: fire the coping prompt before the window opens, learned from their own logs and the clock.
One-tap panic button: screen lock plus a competing somatic action (paced breathing, cold, sharp sensory input). User-initiated, works without the app seeing anything.
Consensual accountability partner — the only monitoring that's both ethical and effective. The user picks the person and picks what's shared (e.g. "I opened a greylisted app," not screenshots). It maps to the peer-support evidence in your docs. The line that keeps it the right side of spyware: the user controls it and can see exactly what's shared.
Keep the app's home turf the support side — urge-surf, values board, the anti-AVE consistency tracking. That's where you move the needle.
